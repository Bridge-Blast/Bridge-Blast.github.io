Blast Bridge.

The Blast Bridge moves ETH and stablecoins from Ethereum to Blast, an Ethereum layer-2 where bridged ETH and USDB can earn native yield automatically. You review the route, the fee and the amount received before you sign, pay gas in ETH, and your funds stay in your own wallet the whole time.

How to use the Blast Bridge

Connect a wallet, choose the asset and amount, and confirm. To use the Blast Bridge you select ETH or a stablecoin on Ethereum, review the route, the fee and the amount that will arrive on Blast, then approve and sign. Assets appear on the L2 after the source transaction confirms, and nothing moves until you sign.

Blast Bridge fees and time

The Blast Bridge costs the Ethereum gas to send plus any protocol fee; transacting on Blast afterwards is cheap like other L2s. Deposits settle after Ethereum confirmations, usually minutes; standard withdrawals back to Ethereum follow the rollup's challenge window. The quote shows the amount received before you commit.

What you can bridge to Blast

You can bridge ETH and stablecoins such as USDC, received on Blast as ETH and USDB, where they can accrue native yield while they sit in your wallet. The interface lists the assets a given route supports and the expected amount for each.

Is the Blast Bridge safe to use?

A self-custody bridge keeps your keys in your wallet. Confirm the destination token's contract address, the amount received and the approval you grant, prefer a limited approval, and keep a little ETH for gas on Blast. Treat any seed-phrase request as fraud.

Native Blast Bridge vs third-party routes

The native Blast Bridge is the canonical deposit route from Ethereum; third-party bridges can be faster for withdrawals or reach more assets, at the cost of another contract and fee. Judge any route by the net amount received and prefer audited options with deep liquidity.
Do I need ETH to use the Blast Bridge?
Yes. You pay Ethereum gas to deposit, and you need a little ETH on Blast to transact once your assets arrive.
How long does the Blast Bridge take?
Deposits usually take minutes after Ethereum confirmations. Standard withdrawals back to Ethereum follow the rollup's challenge window and take longer.
What is USDB on Blast?
USDB is the stablecoin you receive on Blast when you bridge supported stablecoins; like bridged ETH, it can earn native yield.
Do I need an account to bridge to Blast?
No. You connect a self-custody wallet and bridge on-chain, with no sign-up and no custody.
What happens if my deposit fails?
A failed Ethereum transaction reverts and you keep your assets, though the gas for the attempt is spent.
