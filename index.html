How the Blast Bridge works

The Blast Bridge locks your ETH or stablecoin on Ethereum and mints the matching balance on Blast, an Ethereum layer-2 where that balance can start earning native yield automatically. You connect a self-custody wallet, pick the asset and the amount, and the interface shows the fee and the amount that will arrive before you sign anything. Once you approve, the deposit transaction goes to Ethereum, and after it confirms your balance shows up on the L2, usually within minutes. Withdrawals run the other way: you start the exit on Blast, wait out the rollup's challenge window, then claim the asset back on Ethereum. Nothing about the process touches a custodian — the contracts move the asset, and your keys never leave your wallet.

Why Blast pays yield on bridged assets

Blast routes the yield its underlying protocols generate back to the ETH and stablecoin balances sitting on the chain, so assets that cross the Blast Bridge begin accruing automatically rather than sitting idle the way a balance on most other L2s would. You do not stake or lock anything separately — holding bridged ETH or USDB in your wallet is enough.

What happens on each side of a transfer

On Ethereum, the bridge contract receives your deposit and emits a message Blast's network picks up. On the L2, that message mints the equivalent balance to your address — ETH arrives as ETH, and supported stablecoins arrive as USDB. The reverse path burns the asset on Blast and releases it on Ethereum once the exit has finalized.

Blast Bridge fees and timing

A Blast Bridge deposit costs the Ethereum gas needed to run the transaction, plus any small protocol fee the route adds; the quote shows the exact amount you will receive before you commit. Transacting on Blast itself afterward is cheap, in line with other L2s, since execution happens off Ethereum's base layer. Timing is not symmetric: deposits settle a few minutes after Ethereum confirms your transaction, while withdrawals back to Ethereum wait out the rollup's challenge window, which takes longer. What you can bridge to Blast

You can bridge ETH and major stablecoins such as USDC, which arrive on Blast as ETH and USDB. Both sides keep the asset usable immediately — bridged ETH pays gas on the L2, and USDB spends like any stablecoin while it earns yield in the background. The interface lists exactly which assets a given route supports and the amount each one will arrive as, so you know before you sign whether you are receiving USDB or a wrapped token.

ETH on Blast

ETH that crosses to Blast keeps its role as the network's gas asset, so the same balance you move for yield also covers your transaction fees once you start using the L2.

USDB and other stablecoins

Stablecoins such as USDC convert to USDB on arrival. USDB is designed to earn its native yield automatically, so a stablecoin that moves across keeps working for you rather than sitting flat in your wallet.

Blast Bridge step by step

The flow is identical every time you use it, which is what makes a Blast Bridge transfer predictable once you have done it once.
  • Connect a self-custody wallet and select Ethereum as the source, Blast as the destination.
  • Choose ETH or a supported stablecoin and enter the amount.
  • Review the fee and the amount that will arrive on Blast, then approve the token if needed.
  • Sign the deposit transaction and pay Ethereum gas.
  • Wait for Ethereum confirmations — the balance appears on Blast a few minutes later, already earning yield.
  • To exit, start a withdrawal on Blast, wait for the challenge window, then claim on Ethereum.

Is the Blast Bridge safe to use?

A self-custody Blast Bridge never takes control of your assets — the contracts move value between chains while your wallet keeps the keys the entire time. The risks you actually control sit at the moment you sign: the destination contract address, the amount you are set to receive, and the approval you grant.

Approvals worth checking

Grant a limited approval rather than an unlimited one, so a contract can only ever move the amount you intend, and revoke any old allowance you no longer use. Fewer standing approvals mean less surface for any single exploit to reach.

The one rule that never changes

No bridge, wallet or support channel ever needs your seed phrase. A real Blast Bridge only asks you to sign a transaction you can read in your own wallet — it never asks you to type or paste your recovery words anywhere, and any request that does is fraud.

Earning native yield once you're on Blast

Once ETH or USDB lands on the L2, it starts accruing native yield without any extra staking step — the balance sitting in your wallet is the position. From there you can also use Blast's DeFi apps, lending markets and liquidity pools, which layer additional return on top of the base yield if you choose to put the asset to work rather than hold it passively. Keep a small ETH balance on the L2 for gas so a swap or a future withdrawal never stalls for want of fee money.

Native Blast Bridge vs third-party routes

The native Blast Bridge is the canonical deposit path from Ethereum and the one that mints the official representation of your asset on the L2. Third-party bridges can be faster for withdrawals, since they front liquidity instead of waiting on the challenge window, and some reach assets the native route does not support — but each one adds its own contract and its own fee. Judge any route, native or third-party, by the net amount received after gas and fees, and prefer options with deep liquidity and a track record of audits. For a first deposit or a long-term position, the native route remains the simplest choice.
Do I need ETH to use the Blast Bridge?
Yes. You pay Ethereum gas to deposit, and you need a little ETH on Blast to transact once your assets arrive. The amount needed is small since activity on the L2 is cheap.
How long does a transfer to Blast take?
Deposits usually take minutes after Ethereum confirmations. Standard withdrawals back to Ethereum follow the rollup's challenge window and take noticeably longer.
What is USDB on Blast?
USDB is the stablecoin you receive when you bridge supported stablecoins such as USDC. Like bridged ETH, it earns native yield automatically while it sits in your wallet.
Do I need an account to bridge to Blast?
No. You connect a self-custody wallet and bridge on-chain, with no sign-up and no custody at any point in the process.
Why does bridged ETH earn yield on Blast?
Blast directs the yield its underlying protocols generate back to the ETH and USDB balances held on the chain, so a balance that crosses the Blast Bridge starts accruing without any separate staking action.
Is a third-party bridge ever better than the native route?
It can be faster for a withdrawal or reach an asset the native route does not support, since it fronts liquidity instead of waiting on the challenge window. Compare it on the net amount received and the liquidity provider's track record before choosing it over the canonical path.
What happens if my deposit fails?
A failed Ethereum transaction reverts and you keep your assets, though the gas spent on the attempt is lost. Nothing is credited on Blast until the deposit confirms.
